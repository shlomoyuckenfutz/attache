# Monday 11/9 SPY Post-Market Write-Up

Oh boy are we playing with fire. I just spent the last hour going over the charts and numbers over and over again looking for some sign of a real bullish reversal that I may have missed. I came up empty handed every time. To me this still looks like a textbook dead cat bounce rally to cool off oversold conditions before the next leg down. Before I get too far into talking about what I save for my take at the end, lets jump into the charts from today.
Pretty broad based rally today in SPY (unless you were NVDA). +2.37% close brings us to the 61.8% retracement of Thursday's move, not an uncommon bounce target for these kind of rallies. I'm afraid that's where the sunshine and rainbows ends. We topped out today with a rejection of the 2BLN dark pool that came in last Monday at 755.62 and along the way also rejected the 1.9BLN dark pool at 754.51 from last Wednesday. So far this was just a retest of the levels we lost on Thursday, and so far all rejected. Red flag. We also rejected that double dark pool level at 754.05/754.07. At the start of the day we did however test and hold the horizontal support level at 741.26 and broke through the horizontal support level at 746.77, but keep in mind these horizontal support/resistance levels are based on prior consolidation and turning point areas that got hit multiple times, so they are not as strong as the dark pools since there is no large blocks of liquidity parked at those levels. 
In terms of the moving averages, it isn't great, we tried but couldn't make it to the 10DMA. The 20DMA is plunging and widening the gap between it and the 50DMA making it much more difficult to force them into a golden cross. As of right now we are still under every major moving average except the 200DMA. So when I say it is premature to call the bottom, this is what I am talking about. Until we reclaim the 100DMA and 50DMA we are going to be faced with mounting downward pressure. 
The oscillators are again a mixed bag, but it's one of those trap setups. We have a white bar on the MACD and a bullish touch on the RSI. However the stochastic is continuing to cycle down and the AO gave us another red bar today. The last time we had this setup, was last Wednesday. Let me repeat that, the last time we had this same setup was Wednesday, and that was actually less bearish indicating than this one because even though the stochastic was cycling down then it was above the signal line, today it is below. 
 
The hourly offers no comfort as it is already starting to roll over on all the oscillators and that final hour rejection also doesn't inspire much confidence. Now we do still have a little bit of room on the RSI to form a divergence, but with CPI tomorrow it's a coin flip. 
 
The MOC was a nothingburger and ended very close to even.
Gamma is not doing what it normally did when we got these ripping reversals (INCLUDING APRIL), which was swing wildly from very negative to very positive. In fact there are still no call walls > 1BLN. While most of the put walls we had on Friday were eaten away today, the put wall at 730 remained steady at -1.2BLN. Red flag. To add insult to injury, both those call walls at 752 and 753 are all 0dte that are rolling off before the open tomorrow. Looking at the heatmaps for spot volume and open interest we still have very large nodes at 730 for Friday and 725 for 11/20 (Monthly OpEx). Both got a lot of volume action today as well. So it would appear the big guys reloaded their puts here and still think we see 725 by OpEx. 
 
 
The 10 Year Yield and the DXY are both consolidating ahead of tomorrow's data drop. The 10 Year is flirting with the 4.885% level again. The difference now is the regime has changed and higher yields correlate to higher stocks. The DXY meanwhile has effectively gone nowhere for an entire month. It looks like it is trying to avoid a bullish cross of the 10 and 20DMAs but CPI will affect it one way or the other tomorrow, so if the print is hot the DXY will be unable to avoid that bullish cross. 
 
 
Gold on the other hand is still in a transition phase, unsure how it wants to correlate. While it was up today with stocks, it was up on Thursday too. In fact right now we are flirting with Thursday's high and are just threading the 4100 line. So no clear signal here.
 
This brings us to the VIX. -22.24%, wow, so I think it is very clear that most of today's rally came from the VIX crushing. So that means we're out of the woods and the spike is over right? Well for the time being no. The VIX1D came all the way down to the center line and bounced, never once crossing into the compression zone. It ended the day above the 16.08 line which means we are still in a longer duration volatility spike. It also left a down gap which is a big no no for the VIX1D, it does not like to leave gaps open. While the VIX3M/VIX spread did widen by 9 bps today, it is still very much within the downtrend indicating a rising volatility regime is still in effect. This may have just been a back test before spiking again. The other warning sign confirming that we won't see continued vol crush is on the futures term structure the front month is still in backwardation and above spot. Both are not things you see when the VIX is normalizing. 
 
 
 
 

For CPI tomorrow we will only be getting month over month numbers because of the convenient excuse of the shutdown last year. The reason I am calling BS on that is there should have been a workaround. I mean are we just supposed to never get an October year over year print ever again? I'll leave that question to the economists at the BLS. Anyway I digress. Here is a cheat sheet from Financial Juice. We are still in the regime where hot is bad and cool is good due to Fed hiking probabilities being the main concern. The one thing a hot CPI has the potential to break tomorrow is the positive correlation between yields and stocks, as you can see the expected reaction would shove us back into a negative correlation, but we need to just take it as a one off until we see a few more sessions of it in a negative correlation before we can declare the regime changed. Until proven otherwise I will treat such an instance as a divergence. Based on all the data I am seeing it should come in very slightly hotter than expected, but you never can tell with this BLS. Then you also have the wildcard of this being the first release since Trump got obliterated in the midterms, so that begs the question, does this mean they start releasing the real numbers now?
 

My take today, it really all depends on CPI tomorrow. If we get a hot or in line CPI then I see us coming back to give the 200DMA an actual touch. A cool print has the potential to send us up to 760 for a re-test of the 100DMA. That will be the inflection point if that happens as the behavior there will be the tell as to whether or not it's an opportunity for puts, or to open more calls. I would want to see a clean breakout that threatens to retake the 20DMA to get bulled up. As long as the 20/50DMAs are in a bearish arrangement I treat every rally as suspect until proven otherwise, and there is a higher bar for opening upside positions. I have no lean today, but these are the potential paths based on what happens with CPI. If you forced me to anecdotally speculate, and take this with a grain of salt, I would say my expectations lean more toward a bearish outcome here, but I will happily change my mind if the numbers support it.  Right now there are too many factors against this rally, and if there was no CPI print tomorrow I would say this is ready to roll back over by Thursday as it is. This is why the best thing you can do with these data prints is not to play. Wait for the data to come out and confirm, then position with the trend. Otherwise it's just gambling.
